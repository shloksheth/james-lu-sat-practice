Your study sequence

  1. Try each flashcard aloud before revealing it. Recall the rule and an example.
  2. Work through Foundations (1–10), Application (11–20), then Challenge (21–32). Write the sentence skeleton before checking.
  3. Use Study mode for immediate feedback. Choose Test mode to hide answers until you finish the full set.
  4. Review missed or uncertain questions and cards. Retry them tomorrow, in three days, and in a week.

For a later timed attempt, try all 32 questions in 24 minutes. This is a training target, not an official SAT timing rule. Difficulty labels are editorial estimates; this pack does not predict an SAT score.

The solving routine and shortcut guardrails

Inspect the choices; check for names, dash pairs, question marks, and complex lists. Find the main subject and finite verb. Bracket genuine insertions. Classify both sides of the blank. Use CPIN as a checking order: colon; period/semicolon/comma + FANBOYS; isolated comma; no punctuation. Confirm the rule and read the entire completed sentence.

  • A colon needs a complete left clause and an explanation on the right.
  • Removing a phrase must preserve the intended identification, not merely leave a grammatical sentence.
  • An integrated profession + name stays together; a supplementary name can take paired commas.
  • A following because-clause usually stays connected without a comma.
  • Periods and semicolons can differ because of lists or capitalization.
  • Place a transition according to the relationship between the actual statements.
  • A checking priority or an observed answer frequency cannot prove an answer.
