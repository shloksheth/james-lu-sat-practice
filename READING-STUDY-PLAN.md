# James Lu + Khan Academy Reading Study Plan
Checked 2026-10-05. 50 original questions: 27 guided and 23 reserved for a mixed assessment.

Use the two core James Lu reading videos as strategy lessons; use Khan for the full skill progression. The transitions lesson and required grammar bridge complete the Reading and Writing scope.

## Study sequence
Complete one module per session. Suggested session lengths are estimates; allow extra time for error review. Start with 8 Medium Khan questions. If you miss more than 1, do 8 Foundations questions and revisit the lesson. Then attempt 8 Advanced questions; aim for 7/8 or better on fresh questions. Complete 3 original hard questions and explain all distractors. Attempt 4 Challenge questions when Advanced is dependable. Recheck 2 fresh Advanced questions after 24–48 hours.

- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:central-ideas-and-details-2/e/central-ideas-and-details-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:central-ideas-and-details-3/e/central-ideas-and-details-advanced)

Read the question first: a main-idea task asks about the passage as a whole; a detail task asks for one specific relationship. After reading, write a one-sentence summary containing the subject and what the author says about it.
Track any turn in the argument. A passage can introduce an explanation and then limit it, or describe evidence and stop short of a conclusion. Your summary needs that qualification. A true fact from the first sentence can still be a poor main idea if it ignores the rest.
For a detail, return to the relevant statement and paraphrase it. Compare subject, action, condition, time, and degree with the answer. “Some,” “only,” “may,” and “all” change a claim. Keep pronouns attached to the correct people or objects.

Suppose a passage says an archive’s dates conflict, a newly found invoice supports one date, and the invoice’s origin is uncertain. The main point is that new evidence favors a date but remains inconclusive—not that the date has been proven.

Common traps: A correct small detail presented as the whole point; An answer that removes a qualification; An interesting explanation the author never gives
Can you state the main claim and its qualification in one sentence?

## 2. Words in context — about 50 minutes
Choose the precise meaning that the local argument requires.
James Lu supplies a general passage-processing method here; Khan provides the dedicated instruction for this skill.

- [James Lu: Checking each answer's claims against the text (15:15–19:11)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBECQxDBQQs&t=915s)

- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:words-in-context/v/words-in-context-worked-example)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:words-in-context/e/words-in-context-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:words-in-context-2/e/words-in-context-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:words-in-context-3/e/words-in-context-advanced)

Distinguish the two forms: supplying a missing word and interpreting a word already in the passage. In both, read the surrounding statements first. Look for a definition, restatement, example, contrast, or limitation that constrains the meaning.
Predict a simple phrase before checking the choices, such as “limited,” “made less certain,” or “continued despite obstacles.” For a blank, check part of speech and the sentence’s positive, negative, or neutral stance. For an existing word, replace it in the sentence to test its contextual sense.
Reject a near-synonym if it changes intensity, direction, or the object affected. “Questioned” need not mean “disproved”; “qualified” can mean “limited a claim,” not “trained.” Learn unfamiliar words from missed questions with a definition and your own contrasting example.

“The new analysis tempered the researchers’ confidence: several measurements were too noisy to distinguish the two models.” Predict “reduced or moderated,” then reject “created,” “confirmed,” or “completely destroyed.”

Common traps: Choosing the fanciest unfamiliar word; Using a common dictionary meaning that does not fit this sentence; Matching tone while missing precise meaning
Can you point to the phrase that constrains the word and explain why a near-synonym fails?

Explain what a text or selected sentence does in the argument.
Use James Lu’s process, then practice the specific skill with Khan.

- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:text-structure-and-purpose/e/text-structure-and-purpose-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:text-structure-and-purpose-2/e/text-structure-and-purpose-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:text-structure-and-purpose-3/e/text-structure-and-purpose-advanced)

For overall structure, label the moves in order: question, competing explanation, observation, evidence, limitation, conclusion. Turn those labels into a sequence. Avoid simply retelling the topic.
For an underlined portion, inspect what comes before and after it. Ask how the argument would change without it. It might introduce a hypothesis, give an example, provide evidence, acknowledge a limit, or explain why a result matters.
Check every verb in a choice. “Illustrates” is weaker than “proves”; “challenges” differs from “supports”; “introduces” differs from “refutes.” A choice must get both the content and its role right. An author’s purpose need not be a broad persuasive mission.

A sentence reporting an early date can introduce evidence for reconsidering a chronology. If the next sentence says that evidence is not decisive, the role is “motivates a qualified reconsideration,” not “proves the chronology false.”

Common traps: Describing the sentence’s topic instead of its function; Calling a qualified possibility proof; Reversing which explanation is challenged
Can you describe the selected sentence’s role using an action verb?

## 4. Cross-text connections — about 55 minutes
Compare the authors’ precise claims without merging their voices.
James Lu supplies a general passage-processing method here; Khan provides the dedicated instruction for this skill.

- [James Lu: Build a coherent model of the passage (11:28–15:08)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dUl1Hmgx-NE&t=688s)
- [Khan lesson](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:cross-text-connections/a/cross-text-connections-lesson)
- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:cross-text-connections/v/cross-text-connections-video)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:cross-text-connections/e/cross-text-connections-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:cross-text-connections-2/e/cross-text-connections-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-craft-and-structure/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:cross-text-connections-3/e/cross-text-connections-advanced)

Give each text a separate one-sentence claim, including its level of certainty. Then find the shared question. Do not assume disagreement just because the passages discuss different evidence.
For a response question, take the target claim from Text 1 and evaluate it using Text 2’s position. A writer can accept an observation but dispute its explanation, agree with a conclusion but narrow its scope, or say the evidence is insufficient.
Choose the most limited response the second text supports. For an agreement question, the statement must fit both authors. Keep ownership clear: record which writer proposes, observes, doubts, or qualifies each claim.

Text 1 says a revision encouraged readers to infer connections. Text 2 reports that the author intentionally removed connecting phrases. The second text supports that explanation; it does not establish how every reader reacted.

Common traps: Attributing Text 1’s belief to Text 2; Inventing an extreme disagreement; Treating a shared topic as a shared conclusion
Can you state each author’s claim separately and name their exact relationship?

Draw the smallest conclusion the stated facts warrant.
Use James Lu’s process, then practice the specific skill with Khan.

- [James Lu: Prediction, engagement, and small-word traps (15:08–18:24)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dUl1Hmgx-NE&t=908s)
- [Khan lesson](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:inferences/a/inferences-lesson)
- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:inferences/v/inferences-worked-example)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:inferences/e/inferences-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:inferences-2/e/inferences-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:inferences-3/e/inferences-advanced)

Separate what is given from what is implied. For a completion task, identify the conclusion the preceding evidence points toward. A valid inference may use new wording, but each substantive claim still needs support.
Match the strength of the conclusion to the evidence. An association does not establish causation. A failed test can rule out an explanation without proving its replacement. Missing records do not prove that an event never happened.
For scientific or comparative passages, record the groups, condition, measured outcome, and direction of the difference. Preserve “only when,” “unless,” and other restrictions. A plausible story that needs an extra assumption is weaker than a directly supported conclusion.

If two plants grow equally well in sheltered conditions but occupy different field habitats, their field distribution cannot be explained solely by one plant’s inability to grow in shelter. That does not identify the actual cause.

Common traps: Adding an unstated causal explanation; Reversing the comparison; Turning “may” or “some” into “must” or “all”
Can you connect the conclusion to specific premises without adding a new assumption?

## 6. Command of textual evidence — about 75 minutes
Find evidence that tests the full claim, including comparisons and conditions.
Use James Lu’s process, then practice the specific skill with Khan.

- [Khan lesson](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-textual/a/command-of-evidence-textual-lesson)
- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-textual/v/command-of-evidence-scientific-worked-example)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-textual/e/command-of-textual-evidence-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-textual-2/e/command-of-textual-evidence-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-textual-3/e/command-of-textual-evidence-advanced)

Translate the hypothesis into a testable prediction before looking at choices. Record who or what is compared, under which condition, what outcome is measured, and what result would support or weaken the claim.
Support makes that exact claim better grounded; weaken makes it less well grounded. A finding about one group alone may not establish a between-group comparison. If a claim predicts an interaction—an effect under one condition but not another—look for evidence about both conditions.
For literary quotation questions, identify the specific attitude, relationship, or change the quotation must show. A sentence mentioning the right character or setting is not enough. Choose the line that directly demonstrates the claimed behavior or attitude.

Claim: a difficult typeface improves recall only with ample study time. Strong support compares familiar and difficult typefaces in both ample-time and limited-time conditions. “Readers found it difficult” measures difficulty rather than recall.

Common traps: Measuring a different outcome; Supporting only half of a conditional claim; Comparing within a group when the claim compares groups; Treating any relevant quotation as evidence for the precise claim
Can you write the predicted finding before seeing the answers?

## 7. Command of quantitative evidence — about 70 minutes
Use a table or graph to evaluate the stated claim.
James Lu supplies a general passage-processing method here; Khan provides the dedicated instruction for this skill.

- [James Lu: Read the task first and track the exact hypothesis (3:19–8:06)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dUl1Hmgx-NE&t=199s)
- [Khan lesson](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-quantitative/a/command-of-evidence-quantitative-lesson)
- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-quantitative/v/command-of-quantitative-evidence-reading-and-writing-test-sat-khan-academy)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-quantitative/e/command-of-quantitative-evidence-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-quantitative-2/e/command-of-quantitative-evidence-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-information-and-ideas/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:command-of-evidence-quantitative-3/e/command-of-quantitative-evidence-advanced)

Read the title, axis labels, units, legend, and any notes. Identify what one row, point, or bar represents. Then restate the verbal claim as an exact comparison to locate in the graphic.
Distinguish level from change and absolute change from proportional change. A value rising from 2 to 4 and another rising from 20 to 22 have equal absolute changes but different percentage changes. Do only the arithmetic needed for the question.
A numerical answer must satisfy the claim, not merely quote a correct number. Check every subgroup and time period mentioned. Do not infer unmeasured values, causes, or significance. If uncertainty ranges are supplied, respect what they do and do not establish.

Untreated germination times are 14 and 12 days; treated times are 8 and 10. The treatment reduces time by 6 days for A and 2 for B. Comparing only the treated values does not demonstrate which reduction is larger.

Common traps: Comparing endpoints instead of changes; Confusing percentages with percentage points; Reading the wrong row or group; Claiming causation or certainty that the graphic cannot establish
Can you name the exact rows or points that test the claim, and show the needed calculation?

## 8. Transitions — about 55 minutes
Select the exact logical relationship between the relevant statements.
Use James Lu’s process, then practice the specific skill with Khan.

- [Khan worked-example video](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-expression-of-ideas-and-standard-english-conventions/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:transitions/v/transitions-worked-example)
- [Khan Foundations practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:foundations-expression-of-ideas-and-standard-english-conventions/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:transitions/e/transitions-foundations)
- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-expression-of-ideas-and-standard-english-conventions/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:transitions-2/e/transitions-medium)
- [Khan Advanced practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:advanced-expression-of-ideas-and-standard-english-conventions/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:transitions-3/e/transitions-advanced)

Read the complete short passage and identify the propositions connected by the blank. Summarize their relationship before choosing a word: addition, similarity, contrast, concession, example, restatement, cause and effect, sequence, or correction.
Check direction. A specific case can illustrate a preceding general claim; the reverse arrangement may require a different phrase. A contrast word is inappropriate when the second sentence simply narrows or clarifies the first. An expectation followed by a surprising result often creates concession.
For phrases such as “challenging this assumption,” identify the exact referent of “this.” For choices within one category, test their meaning rather than eliminating both automatically. Finally, substitute the choice and reread the connected statements. Logical transition choice differs from punctuating a transition.

“The archive search found several letters. None mentioned the work under investigation.” There is a useful discovery followed by a limit on its usefulness. A contrast or concession fits; a cause-and-effect transition would invent a relationship.

Common traps: Deciding from one sentence alone; Choosing an example word merely because a name appears; Confusing restatement with consequence; Ignoring the noun or claim referred to by “this”
Can you explain the relationship without using any of the proposed transition words?

- [Khan Medium practice](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/sat-reading-and-writing/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:medium-expression-of-ideas-and-standard-english-conventions/x0d47bcec73eb6c4b:rhetorical-synthesis-2/e/rhetorical-synthesis-medium)

Read the goal before reading the notes: introduce a person to an unfamiliar audience, compare two methods, emphasize a similarity, illustrate a trend, or explain an advantage. The goal determines which facts matter.
Select the minimum relevant notes, then check the choice against both the goal and the facts. A true sentence can still fail if it discusses a different feature, provides only one side of a comparison, or gives an audience details it does not need.
Keep relationships precise: a date beside a discovery does not establish a causal connection, and a difference in output does not automatically establish superiority. There is no need to use all the notes. Avoid adding facts, evaluations, or generalizations not supported by them.

If the goal is to emphasize two projects’ shared use of recycled materials, choose a sentence explicitly linking both projects to that feature. Their different launch dates may be accurate but irrelevant to this goal.

Common traps: Accurate information that misses the requested goal; A comparison mentioning only one item; An unsupported evaluation such as “best”; Using every note and obscuring the main point
Can you name the goal, relevant notes, and the phrase in the answer that fulfills it?

## Final check
Take the 23-question original mixed reading assessment without opening explanations. Aim for at least 85% and inspect results by skill; a strong total must not conceal a weak category. Then use a fresh Khan Course challenge, which includes grammar, and complete the grammar unit. A Bluebook Reading and Writing practice section is the final check when you are ready to use a saved practice test. Completing videos alone does not establish readiness.
